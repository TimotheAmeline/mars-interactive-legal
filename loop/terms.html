4. Health and Safety (Photosensitivity Warning)

Please read this section before playing, and make sure any child who plays Loop has it explained to them.

A very small percentage of people may experience a seizure when exposed to certain visual images, including flashing lights or patterns that may appear in video games. Even people with no history of seizures or epilepsy may have an undiagnosed condition that can cause these "photosensitive epileptic seizures" while playing. Loop features bright neon visuals on a dark background, continuously cycling colors, fast motion, and flashing, shattering, and glitching effects.

If you or anyone in your family has an epileptic condition or has ever had a seizure, consult a doctor before playing. Stop playing immediately and consult a doctor if you experience any of the following: dizziness, altered vision, eye or face twitching, jerking or shaking of arms or legs, disorientation, confusion, momentary loss of awareness, or any involuntary movement. Parents should watch for, or ask their children about, these symptoms — children and teenagers are more likely than adults to experience them.

To reduce the risk: play in a well-lit room, hold your device at a comfortable distance from your eyes, avoid playing when you are tired or drowsy, and take a 10 to 15 minute break every hour. If your hands, wrists, or eyes become tired or sore while playing, stop and rest before playing again.
