One Tap. One Wire. One More Run.

Loop is a neon arcade game about staying on the wire. You ride a glowing loop that keeps bending and stretching under you, with two lanes — inner and outer — and a single input: tap to switch. That's the entire control scheme. Everything else is timing, nerve, and reading the track a split second before it reads you.

Every lap is faster than the last. Spikes sit in your lane, and you have to be in the other one when they arrive. Black holes pull at the rail and swallow you whole, shield or no shield. Warp holes open a bonus round where nothing can hurt you and every spike you ride into pays. And once in a run, the world glitches, shatters, and hands you a completely different view of the same track — keep your rhythm or snap.

Loop takes seconds to pick up and is very hard to put down. Your run lasts exactly as long as you do, and your best is always one more loop away.

Core Features:

  • One-tap controls. Tap anywhere to switch lanes. No virtual joystick, no button layout to learn, no menus between you and the next run.
  • A track that fights back. Static spikes, stacked stings, moving hazards, black holes that one-shot you through any shield, and a rare warp hole that opens a bonus round: no danger, a combo that can't break, and points for every spike you smash.
  • Pickups that change the run. SHIELD absorbs a hit, SLOW bends time around your next three spikes, BOOM breaks every spike in sight, REVERSE flips your direction of travel, and CURSE widens you and slows your lane switch for a while. Orbs sit on the danger lane, and most of them cost points and break your combo, so grabbing one is always a choice.
  • Combo scoring. Every spike you dodge in a row pays more than the last, and every completed loop pays +100. Greed and survival pull in opposite directions.
  • Five modes, five leaderboards. Original (one hit and you're done), Easy(R) (three lives, and losing one slows the ride down), FTL (it speeds up fast, and it's the hardest mode), Purist (spikes and holes only, no pickups), and Steady (the speed stays constant while the obstacles keep coming). Each keeps its own top-10, right on your device, with the developer's own best (the Creator score) in there for you to beat.
  • A once-per-run glitch shift. Mid-run, the world tears apart and reassembles from a different camera. Same track, new eyes. (Purist keeps it pure: no shift, no warp.)
  • A soundtrack generated as you play. The music and sound effects are synthesized live on your device and react to what's happening on the wire, with separate toggles for sound, effects, and music, plus haptics on your phone.
  • Learn as you go. The first time you meet something new, time slows down and a short tip tells you what it is. Turn tips off whenever you like.
  • Two looks. A dark neon theme and a bright white one, one tap apart.
  • Fully offline. No connection needed, ever. Loop plays exactly the same on a plane as it does on your couch.

Loop is the one-more-run game for the thirty seconds you have, and the thirty minutes you didn't mean to spend.

Your Privacy, Protected:

  • Zero tracking.
  • Zero ads.
  • Zero data harvesting.
  • Zero accounts, zero online leaderboards, zero internet connection, by design — your scores never leave your device.

The same design means Loop has no cloud save of its own: your scores live on the device you play on, and uninstalling the game removes them. And because Loop is a game made of light, it uses bright, fast-changing neon colors and flashing effects. If you or anyone in your family has a history of epilepsy or photosensitivity, please read the Health and Safety section of the Terms of Use before playing.
